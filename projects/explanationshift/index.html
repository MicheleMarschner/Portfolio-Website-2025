01 / RESEARCH QUESTIONS

When Predictions Stay Stable, Do Explanations?

Explainable AI methods help us understand which parts of an input influence a model's prediction. But how reliable are these explanations when the input distribution changes?

This research investigates three questions:

  • How does explanation drift change as distribution shifts become more severe?
  • Can explanations change substantially even when model predictions remain correct?
  • How do explanation-quality metrics respond to distribution shift?

02 / EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN & IMPLEMENTATION

Controlled Distribution Shifts

I independently designed and implemented an experimental pipeline to investigate how model predictions and visual explanations change under distribution shift. By pairing clean CIFAR-10 images with their corrupted counterparts from CIFAR-10-C, I could compare predictions and attributions for the same image under different conditions.

  • Model: ResNet-18 trained on CIFAR-10
  • Data: CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-10-C
  • Corruptions: Gaussian noise, defocus blur, brightness, and fog
  • Explanation methods: GradCAM and Integrated Gradients, implemented with Captum
  • Evaluation: Predictive behaviour, explanation drift, and explanation-quality metrics using Quantus

Experiments were repeated across multiple corruption severity levels and five random seeds. To distinguish explanation drift from prediction failures, I analyzed all samples alongside subsets with unchanged predictions and predictions that remained correct under corruption. Quantitative results were complemented by qualitative comparisons of attribution maps.

03 / KEY FINDINGS

What Did the Experiments Reveal?

FINDING 01

Explanation Drift Increases with Shift Severity

Both GradCAM and Integrated Gradients show increasing explanation drift as corruption severity rises. However, the magnitude and progression depend on the corruption type and explanation method.

FINDING 02

Correct Predictions Do Not Guarantee Stable Explanations

Substantial explanation drift persists even when predictions remain unchanged and correct. These examples illustrate silent drift: the model's output remains correct, while the attribution changes considerably.

FINDING 03

Explanation Metrics Behave Differently Across Methods

Sparseness responds differently for GradCAM and Integrated Gradients. GradCAM's metric change largely disappears when restricting evaluation to stable, correct predictions, whereas Integrated Gradients retains structured changes.

04 / RESEARCH TAKEAWAYS

Beyond Prediction Accuracy

The experiments demonstrate that stable predictions do not necessarily imply stable explanations. A model may continue to classify an image correctly while its attribution patterns change substantially.

The results also highlight the importance of considering the explanation method and evaluation metric when assessing explanation robustness.

Evaluating model behavior under distribution shift therefore requires looking beyond prediction accuracy alone.
